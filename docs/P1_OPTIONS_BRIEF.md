# P1 Options Brief — Setting Decisions for "The Last Berth"

**Date:** 2026-10-18
**For:** the project owner. These decisions are [APPROVAL REQUIRED] (TECHNICAL_SPEC.md §41,
ART_BIBLE.md §24) and must be made before recruitment (Ticket 5).
**Status:** options and a recommendation. Nothing here is decided until the owner chooses.

## How to read this

**The decisions depend on each other.** A Portuguese sponsor implies a Lisbon departure and a Brazil
or North Atlantic destination. A Castilian royal contract implies Seville and the Caribbean
mainland. So the brief offers **three coherent packages** (§2), then the detail for each decision
(§3–§7).

**How sure each claim is:**
- **Sourced:** backed by the linked source, mostly encyclopedic or secondary.
- **To verify:** plausible, but I didn't confirm it in this pass.

Nothing here is museum-grade yet. ART_BIBLE.md §17 asks for scholarly and museum sources before
final assets, and §9 lists that research per package.

**The game's needs that decide fit:** a small crew (about 6–12 recruits plus the captain), a
dangerous crossing, a plausible chance of shipwreck, a small settlement surviving "two winters",
and Indigenous communities with their own goals rather than enemies or empty land (GAME_VISION.md
§2, §8.3).

**Recommendation: Package B**, with Package A as the strong alternative.

**Why B:**
- **Scale.** The historical model is a *small* crew left to hold a fortified trading post, with a
  ship expected back. That is almost exactly "The Last Berth".
- **Relationships.** Survival depends on the relationship with the neighbouring community: trade,
  labour and food rather than conquest. Your documents require exactly that.
- **Rivals.** French ships on the same coast from about 1503 add a ready-made rival expedition for
  later phases.

## 2. The packages

### Package A — "Tierra Firme": Seville/Sanlúcar to the Gulf of Urabá and Darién, c. 1509–1511

**The story.**
- **Departure:** the captain holds a share in a Crown-licensed venture to the mainland ("Tierra
  Firme").
- **The real precedent (sourced):**
  - Alonso de Ojeda founded San Sebastián de Urabá in 1509 with about 70 men, met armed resistance
    and left.
  - Diego de Nicuesa's larger expedition was shipwrecked and nearly destroyed. The survivors
    refused to let him stay, and he sailed off in 1511 and vanished.
  - The settlement moved across the gulf to found Santa María la Antigua del Darién (1510).
  - Sources: [Santa María la Antigua del Darién](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_la_Antigua_del_Dari%C3%A9n),
    [Alonso de Ojeda](https://www.biografiasyvidas.com/biografia/o/ojeda.htm),
    [Diego de Nicuesa](https://www.biografiasyvidas.com/biografia/n/nicuesa.htm).
- **Why it fits:** few real settings have this much documented shipwreck, starvation, leadership
  crisis and succession. GAME_VISION.md §3.3 asks for exactly these.

**Communities: the Cueva** (sourced).
- **Who they were:** a people of many small chiefdoms. Spanish narratives name the caciques Careta and
  Comogre, with alliances, captures and baptisms.
- **What happened to them:** their language is extinct, with about 50 known words, and their
  population collapsed between 1510 and 1535 under conflict, disease and colonisation.
- **Sources:** [Cueva language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cueva_language),
  [Andagoya's narrative](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Narrative_of_the_Proceedings_of_Pedrarias_Davila/Narrative_of_Pascual_de_Andagoya).
- **The difficulty:** nearly every source was written by the conquerors.

**Risks.**
- **It is a conquest setting.** Depicting it without making Indigenous people "hostile encounter
  units" (ART_BIBLE.md §7.5) is possible, but it is the hardest of the three.
- **The material is thin.** Cueva culture survives in scraps.

### Package B — "The Brazil Factory": Lisbon to a fortified brazilwood trading post, c. 1503–1515 (recommended)

**The story.**
- **The sponsor:** a Lisbon merchant consortium holds the Crown's lease on brazilwood. It sends the
  captain to supply, staff or found a fortified trading post (*feitoria*) on the coast.
- **The lease (sourced):** around 1502 the Crown leased brazilwood to merchants led by Fernão de
  Loronha. The lease required six ships a year and fortified trading posts kept for three years.
  - Sources: [History of Pernambuco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Pernambuco),
    [The Leasing of Brazil, 1502–1515 (Cambridge)](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/americas/article/abs/leasing-of-brazil-15021515-a-problem-resolved/FDD46AF40F74A9668E47A620E4841C0B).
- **The precedent (sourced):** in 1503–1504 Gonçalo Coelho's fleet built a fort, loaded brazilwood,
  and **left 24 men there** when it sailed for Lisbon.
  - **Disputed:** where the post stood (around Cabo Frio / Arraial do Cabo), and whether Vespucci's
    letter describing it is authentic.
  - Source: [Gonçalo Coelho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gon%C3%A7alo_Coelho).
- **How the trade worked (sourced):** Indigenous people cut and carried the logs in exchange for
  metal tools.
  - Sources: [brazilwood trade overview](https://bahia.ws/en/the-brazilwood-trade-and-european-presence-on-the-brazilian-coast/),
    [Brasil dictionary entry (Salamanca)](https://brasilhisdictionary.usal.es/en/palo-brasil-3/).
- **Rivals (sourced):** French ships came to the coast for brazilwood from about 1503, and Norman
  traders built ties with the Tupinambá.
  - Sources: [timeline](https://balagan.info/timeline-for-colonial-brazil),
    [Norman trade study (Unicamp)](https://ojs.ifch.unicamp.br/index.php/modernoscontemporaneos/article/download/4331/3304/13154).

**Why it fits the game.**
- **Scale:** a tiny garrison, a deadline (the ship's return), and limited supplies.
- **Locals as partners:** the community next door is a trading partner and power broker, so
  relationships matter more than fighting.
- **Rivals:** a European rival is present.
- **Shipwreck:** shipwreck and abandonment are plausible branches.
- **Settlement:** two winters of keeping a post alive is a natural charter goal.

**Communities: Tupi-speaking coastal peoples.**
- **To verify:** which people lived at the chosen site. Sources mention the Tupinambá in French
  contact. "Tupiniquim" is often named for parts of the coast, but I couldn't confirm this here.
  Record the choice with sources before any assets are made.
- **Why this is a good basis:** the trade relationship gives Indigenous characters clear interests
  and leverage (labour, food, knowledge of the land, alliances against rivals). This is the agency
  ART_BIBLE.md §7.5 and TECHNICAL_SPEC.md §30 require.

**Risks.**
- **The site is disputed.** Choosing a fictionalised trading post "near Cabo Frio" with documented
  uncertainty is acceptable under ART_BIBLE.md §17.
- **Language specifics.** Tupi language and material culture need specialist sources.
- **History of violence.** Captivity and violence were also part of this history and must not be
  airbrushed.

### Package C — "Land of Cod": Lisbon or Terceira to Newfoundland, c. 1500–1506

**The story (sourced):**
- **The Corte-Real brothers:** Gaspar Corte-Real held a royal charter (1500), reached a "Terra Verde"
  (probably Newfoundland), and on the second voyage (1501) returned with captives, probably Beothuk.
  Gaspar was then lost, and his brother Miguel was lost searching for him (1502).
- **Cod fishing:** Portuguese fishing grew fast; by 1506 Portugal taxed Newfoundland cod. Bristol
  syndicates were also active.
- **Sources:** [Heritage Newfoundland: The Portuguese Explorers](https://www.heritage.nf.ca/articles/exploration/portuguese.php),
  [Library and Archives Canada: the Corte-Real brothers](https://epe.lac-bac.gc.ca/100/206/301/lac-bac/passages-ef/passages/h8-215-e.html),
  [Acadiensis (2011) on the captive numbers](https://www.erudit.org/fr/revues/acadiensis/2011-v40-n1-acad_40_1/acad40_1art02).

**Why it fits:** the most survival-heavy setting. Winter is the enemy, ships go missing, and a cod
station is a believable small foothold.

**Risks.**
- **Little contact to build on.** Beothuk contact was minimal and largely avoided, and the best-known
  early episode is a kidnapping. That leaves little documented ground for the relationship systems the
  game is built around.
- **Thin records for every role.** That makes the recruitment and port content harder too.

### Considered and not recommended

**Florida (Calusa), 1513–1521.**
- **History (sourced):** the Calusa were a powerful fishing chiefdom based at Calos (Mound Key). They
  attacked Ponce de León's fleet in 1513 and defeated his 1521 colony; he died of his wounds.
- **Why not:** a strong story, but it frames the main Indigenous presence almost entirely as armed
  resistance from first contact.
- **Sources:** [Florida Museum: the Calusa](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/rrc/blog/documenting-the-1614-spanish-attack-on-the-calusa-realm/),
  [Archaeology: the Calusa capital](https://archaeology.org/issues/online/collection/florida-calusa-capital).

## 3. Departure port

| Option | Facts (sourced) | Game notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Seville, with the outport of Sanlúcar de Barrameda** (A) | The Casa de Contratación (1503) approved every Indies voyage, licensed pilots and emigrants, and kept Seville's monopoly. Large ships loaded at Sanlúcar, and Magellan sailed from there in 1519. [Casa de Contratación](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_de_Contrataci%C3%B3n) | A natural "customs house / sponsor's office" district (GAME_VISION.md §5.1). Paperwork, licences and deadlines are built into the history. |
| **Palos and Moguer** (A, variant) | The home ports of the Pinzón and Niño seafaring families; Columbus sailed from Palos in 1492. Only about 230 people left Palos for the Indies by 1550. [Niño brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ni%C3%B1o_brothers), [CSM report](https://csmonitor.com/1992/0106/06141.html) | Intimate and small. By the 1500s, Indies voyages needed Seville's licence, so the drama is a small-town crew under distant authority. |
| **Lisbon** (B, C) | The seat of the Crown's overseas trade (the Casa da Índia supervised loading and paid sailors) and the base of the brazilwood consortium. Its archives were largely lost in the 1755 earthquake. [Casa da Índia note](https://eve.fcsh.unl.pt/en/node/5196) | A big, international port. The record gaps mean more documented invention. |

**Recommendation:** Lisbon for Package B, or Sanlúcar/Seville for A. One compact district of the chosen
port is enough for P1 (GAME_VISION.md §10).

## 4. Sponsor and charter

| Option | Facts | Charter goals that fit the game |
|---|---|---|
| **Castilian Crown contract (*capitulación*)** (A) | The Crown granted contracts to people who risked their own capital. It kept sovereignty, took a share (often the *quinto*, one-fifth, of precious metals and loot) and could appoint overseers. Pizarro's 1529 contract also required founding settlements and bringing priests. [Royal fifth](https://EN.wikipedia.com/wiki/Royal_fifth), [Pizarro and Panama](https://simplestudy.com/gb/gcse/edexcel/history/revision-notes/spain-and-the-new-world-c1490-c1555/the-conquistadors-c1513-c1528/pizarro-and-panama). *Terms varied by contract.* | Found and hold a settlement; report to the Crown's officer; obligations the player can break, with consequences. |
| **Portuguese Crown lease to merchants** (B) | The brazilwood lease from about 1502: six ships a year and fortified posts kept for three years (see Package B sources). | Hold the trading post until the ship returns; deliver a cargo quota of brazilwood; keep the peace that the trade depends on. A measurable, data-driven charter (GAME_VISION.md §3.4). |
| **Royal exploration charter to a captain** (C) | Corte-Real's 1500 charter (see Package C sources). | Find and claim; return or winter over. Less of a settlement goal. |

## 5. Hero ship

| Option | Facts | Game notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Caravel** (*caravela*) | Light and handy. Columbus's caravels were roughly 50–100 tons, and caravels stayed in Atlantic and Brazil service after they proved too fragile for the Indian Ocean. [MUCAIN: Ships of the Carrera de Indias](https://mucain.com/en/collection/the-ships-of-the-carrera-de-indias/), [Portuguese caravel note](https://eve.fcsh.unl.pt/en/node/5101) | Fits a crew of 6–12 recruits plus officers. | **Small nao** (carrack) | Larger; the 1492 Santa María was about 100 tons with about 40 crew. [World History: Carrack](https://www.worldhistory.org/Carrack/) | More cargo, more crew, more deck to draw. Better if the owner wants a crew of about 20 or more. |

## 7. Candidate identity fields (for Ticket 5)

What recruitment needs, with evidence:
- **Sex.** Women did sail to the Indies early: four of about 1,500 people on Columbus's second fleet
  (1493), and 30 listed on the third (1498), some of them wives of expedition members.
  [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/women-conquistadoras-spain-america).
  Ship crews were overwhelmingly male.
  *Recommendation:* add a `sex` field now. Women are rare among sailors in recruitment and more
  common among settlers and passengers. That also fixes the "beards for everyone" placeholder.
- **Origin and languages.** Crews were mixed. Foreigners served on Spanish ships from the earliest
  years, and Portuguese sailors were the largest foreign group (sourced, though mostly for the later
  1500s: [Habeler Institute study](https://habeleinstitute.org/w/images/a/a5/ADBHEZ5A.pdf)).
  *Recommendation:* each character gets a list of languages with a 0–5 level. The **interpreter**
  role (a *língua*, someone who learned an Indigenous language) becomes a valuable recruit.
  - **To verify:** the Portuguese practice of leaving convicts (*degredados*) on the coast to learn
    languages. If confirmed, it's a rich candidate background for Package B.
- **Free and enslaved Africans** sailed and served in early Atlantic voyages. *To verify* with
  specific sources before writing any character, for example accounts of free Black
  conquistadors. Represent with care and specificity, never as decoration.
- **Religion and status** matter to contracts and sponsor obligations. A simple `status` (gentleman,
  sailor, craftsman, convict, servant) is enough for P1.

## 9. Research tasks once the owner chooses

For **B**, before content production:
1. **Site.** Pick the trading-post site and its uncertainty, and record the sources.
2. **People.** Identify the Indigenous people and polity at that site around 1511, with scholarly
   sources on settlement, food, trade, warfare, diplomacy and language.
3. **Ship.** Portuguese caravel construction, rig and deck layout around 1510; the ship's boat; the
   pumps.
4. **Wardrobe.** Lisbon port life and sailors' clothing around 1510. This replaces the placeholder
   in `data/appearance.json`.
5. **Supplies.** Provisions and trade goods typical of the brazilwood voyages, such as metal tools and
   biscuit, for `data/items.json`.
6. **French rivals.** Norman ship types and how the French traded, for the rival expedition later.

For **A**, the equivalents: the Urabá and Darién coast, Cueva society (from scholarship, critically
reading the conquest chronicles), Castilian naos, and Seville's Casa procedures.
