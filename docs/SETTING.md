# Setting Outline — "The Last Berth"

**Status:** APPROVED by the owner on 2026-10-18 (D-013). Every name here is a first draft. Change any of
them freely: content IDs in `data/` stay neutral, so renaming costs nothing.
**Approach:** a fictional world, clearly inspired by the early-1500s Atlantic, with the technology,
ships, clothing and daily life of roughly 1490–1530. Inspirations are noted in *italics* so later
work stays coherent. Nothing here claims to be a real place or event.

---

## 1. The world in one paragraph

The Crown of **Castrena** has spent a generation sending ships west across the **Ocean Sea**. Its
river port, **Almara**, grew rich on licences, debts and rumours. Charts are guarded like gold, and
every voyage must pass through the **Chamber of the Ocean Sea**. On the far side lies a long
tropical shore the sailors call the **Ember Coast**, after the red dye-wood that grows there. It is
not empty. The people who live there have watched foreign ships come and go for ten years, and
they have their own opinions about them.

*Inspirations: Castile and Portugal; Seville and Lisbon; the Casa de Contratación; the brazilwood
coast.*

## 2. The Old World

**The Crown of Castrena**
- **What it is:** a southern maritime kingdom that grants charters and leases.
- **What it does:** takes a royal share of every profit, and sends officers to watch its partners.
- **The king:** **Aurelio III**. Dates are written as "the 14th year of King Aurelio", so the
  game never pins down a calendar year.

**Almara, the departure port**
- **The place:** a crowded river city of warehouses and shipyards, where creditors, chapels and
  taverns all compete for sailors.
- **The river problem:** big ships can't come all the way upriver. They load and wait for wind at
  **La Bocana**, the outport at the river mouth, which is where the voyage leaves from.
- *Inspirations: Seville with Sanlúcar de Barrameda, plus Lisbon's quaysides.*

**The Chamber of the Ocean Sea**
- **What it does:** licenses ships, inspects cargo, registers crews, and sets the sailing deadline
  for the season's convoy.
- **In the game:** the "customs house / sponsor's office" district, with paperwork, fees and an
  official who can be bribed or offended.

**Lusara, the rival crown**
- **What it is:** a neighbouring seafaring kingdom with its own claims and its own pilots.
- **Who you meet:** Lusaran sailors are common in Almara and can be recruited, though they're
  distrusted.
- *Inspiration: Portugal as Castile's rival and partner.*

**The Brennish, northern interlopers**
- **Who they are:** traders and occasional raiders from a cold northern coast, with no licence
  from anyone.
- **Their reach:** they trade on the Ember Coast too, and they have friends there.
- *Inspiration: Norman and Breton traders on the Brazil coast.*

**Faith:** "the Church", with its own saints, feast days and chaplains. It's real to the
characters, and kept fictional in its details.

## 3. The sponsor and the charter

**Sponsor: the Ledra–Varzi Company.** A consortium of an Almaran merchant house (Ledra) and a
foreign banking family (Varzi). It holds the Crown's lease on **embrawood**, a red dye-wood worth a
fortune to cloth-dyers.
- **Lease terms:** the lease requires the Company to keep a fortified trading post, a **factory**,
  on the Ember Coast and to deliver a quota of wood each year.
- *Inspirations: the brazilwood lease; Genoese bankers in Seville.*

**The hook: the factory has gone silent.**
- **What happened:** two years ago the Company left a small garrison at **Punta Ceniza** (Ash
  Point). Last season's ship came back without them: it found the post burned and nobody there
  who would talk.
- **The captain's commission:** reach Punta Ceniza, find out what happened, re-establish the
  factory, and hold it.

**The charter.** These become the data-driven win and lose conditions:
- **Sail with this season's convoy.** The convoy leaves La Bocana when the winds turn. Late ships
  lose their licence fee and their sponsor's patience. This is "the last berth".
- **Re-establish the factory** and keep at least N people alive there until the relief ship comes,
  about two seasons later (the "two winters" goal from GAME_VISION.md).
- **Deliver M logs of embrawood** to the relief ship.
- **Keep the trade peace.** The Company forbids raiding or enslaving the coast's people. That's
  partly conscience and mostly that the trade depends on them. Breaking it ends the charter's
  protection.
- **Failure:** losing the ship and the factory, the garrison falling below N, or the Company
  revoking the charter.

**Why this works for the game:**
- It's small and personal: one ship, a dozen people.
- It has a deadline and a mystery.
- Survival depends on relationships, not conquest.
- Shipwreck, mutiny, and a "go native or go home" outcome are all natural branches.

## 4. The ship

**The *Garza* (the Heron)**
- **What she is:** a well-used caravel of about 60 tons, square-rigged on the fore and main masts
  with a lateen sail on the mizzen. Exact rig details are for the plausibility pass.
- **Room:** about 16–20 aboard: the captain, a few fixed officers (pilot, boatswain), and **8–12
  recruited crew**.
- **What she carries:** a ship's boat, two pumps, a galley fire box, and a hold that takes about 2
  tonnes of stores and trade goods.
- **Her past:** two crossings already, a patched hull and a mast with a history. The player
  inspects her before sailing (GAME_VISION.md §5).
- *Inspiration: Portuguese and Castilian caravels on Atlantic and Brazil routes.*

## 5. The far coast

**The Ember Coast** is a long, humid shore:
- **Landscape:** sandbars, river mouths and mangrove lagoons, with dye-wood forest and hills
  behind.
- **Seasons:** two, wet and dry. Storms come at the change of season.
- **Hazards:** reefs off the points, and fresh water that is plentiful upriver but scarce on the
  sand spits.
- **Punta Ceniza:** a rocky headland with the burned factory, a spring, and a lagoon behind it.

### The Ahuneri, the lagoon people

**Who they are:** a coastal people of several villages around the great lagoon. Each village is a
ring of long communal houses.

**How they live:**
- **Food:** gardens of root crops and grain, fish weirs, and shellfish beds.
- **Craft:** expert canoe builders.

**How decisions are made:**
- Each village is governed by a council of household elders. A chosen **speaker** carries the
  council's word, but can't command against it.
- Obligation and status run through gift exchange. A gift unanswered is an insult.

**Their history with foreigners:**
- **Trading the wood:** they controlled the dye-wood stands and traded logs and food to the old
  factory for iron tools.
- **The raid:** a foreign ship once seized people from a fishing camp. That memory is the reason
  for their caution.
- **What they know:** some of them know what happened at Punta Ceniza.

**What they want:** iron tools and cloth; allies against the upriver rival; control of their own
forests; and never again to lose people to foreign ships.

**Internal politics:** two villages disagree about the newcomers. **Mahenu** favours renewed trade;
**Oruwai** favours keeping them weak and at a distance.

**Language sound:** open syllables, soft *h*, *n*, *r* and *w* (Ahuneri, Mahenu, Oruwai, *hawe*).

### The Tesiwa, the river people

**Who they are:** an inland people of the river valleys above the lagoon.

**How they live:** hunters, farmers and makers of salt and fine stone tools. They control the river
route to the interior.

**How decisions are made:** led by war captains chosen for each season or venture, under a hereditary
lineage that keeps the sacred hill. More hierarchical than the Ahuneri.

**Their position:** rivals and sometimes trading partners of the Ahuneri. They resent the lagoon's
hold on foreign trade, and they **have been trading with the Brennish**.

**What they want:** a direct share of the foreigners' goods, to break the Ahuneri monopoly, and
respect for their hill and river.

**Language sound:** closed syllables, *s*, *t*, *k* and long vowels (Tesiwa, Kessat, Tuuk).

**Design rules for both peoples** (ART_BIBLE.md §7.5, D-012):
- **Coherent:** each people's architecture, clothing, tools, food, music and beliefs are designed as
  their own consistent culture. They are not borrowed from one real people, and not a mix of many.
- **Agency:** individuals have names, personalities, ambitions and disagreements, and can become
  persistent characters with full simulation (relationships, knowledge, history).
- **Behaviour:** they act on their own interests. They trade, refuse, test the newcomers, ally,
  bargain and withdraw. Neither people is the "hostile" one or the "friendly" one.
- **Choices:** the player's choices about trade, promises, violence, alliances and the fate of the
  old garrison shape how both peoples respond.

## 6. Recruits and identity (for Ticket 5)

**New character fields:**
- **sex**, recorded as a fact about the character;
- **origin** (town and region);
- **languages**, each with a 0–5 level;
- **status:** gentleman, sailor, craftsman, servant or convict.

**Languages in the setting:**
- **Castrenan** (most crew);
- **Lusaran**;
- **Brennish**;
- **Ligurese**, the merchants' and bankers' tongue;
- **Ahuneri** (rare, and precious);
- **Tesiwa** (almost unknown).

**About ten candidates in Almara:** some obvious, some with hidden qualities. A first pass:
- **A Lusaran pilot** who knows the coast, but whose reasons for wanting to go back are unclear.
- **A barber-surgeon** fleeing debts.
- **A ship's carpenter** too old for heavy work but irreplaceable.
- **A young widow, the Company's clerk,** going to claim her late husband's share. She can read
  the factory's ledgers.
- **A convict** offered a pardon for the voyage.
- **An interpreter (*lengua*)** who claims to have lived among the Ahuneri for a year. Whether that's
  true, and what they owe them, is a hidden quality.
- **A cooper, a cook, and two or three ordinary sailors** with useful quirks.
- **A disgraced gentleman** with money and no sea legs.

Most sailors are men; women appear as passengers, clerks, servants and settlers. The crew is
mixed in origin, and the Company hires whoever signs.

## 7. The first-playable district

For P1 the port is one compact district: **the Bocana waterfront**.
- **the quay,** with the *Garza*;
- **the Chamber's office;**
- **a chandler's and provision market;**
- **the tavern *Three Anchors*,** for rumours, introductions and candidates;
- **a small chapel;**
- **the Company's counting house.**

About 25 people live and work there on schedules (GAME_VISION.md §11).
